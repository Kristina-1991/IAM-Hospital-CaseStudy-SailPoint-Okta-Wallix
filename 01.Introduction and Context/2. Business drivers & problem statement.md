# Business Drivers & Problem Statement 

## Core Clinical Systems supporting the patient Journey

CHR Brussels relies on three mission-critical applications:

- **DXPlanning** – Appointment scheduling, resource allocation system (managing appointment booking, cancellations, resource availability and calendar synchronization). 
It supports efficient patient flow and reduces no-shows through automated reminders, while also interfacing with clinical information systems to ensure seamless access to patient data during scheduling and follow-up with HL7 interoperability.

- **QBLOC** – Modular surgical block planning, operating room coordination, pre/per-operative data entry (such as procedure details,anesthesia and medical consumables), consent tracking, and real-time visualization of operating room status. QBloc ensures rigorous traceability and timestamping of all surgical events, enabling audit compliance and operational performance analysis through integrated reporting and business intelligence tools.

- **OASIS+** – An integrated clinical platform consolidating the full patient electronic record, including inpatient and outpatient clinical documentation, medical history, laboratory results, diagnostic information, and admission details. It enables healthcare providers to access up-to-date comprehensive patient information to support clinical decision-making, continuity of care, and regulatory reporting. The platform supports interoperability standards (such as HL7) enabling smooth exchanges with other hospital systems.

Access to these systems is currently managed manually via IT tickets, with no centralized IAM platform.

## Problem Statement

The absence of a modern IAM ecosystem creates structural vulnerabilities:

- No automated identity lifecycle → Orphan/dormant accounts, delayed deprovisioning, shared credentials.
- Fragmented access policies → Inconsistent roles across systems, no centralized visibility.
- Weak authentication & traceability → Limited MFA, no unified audit trail for patient data access.
- Regulatory exposure → Non-compliance with GDPR (Art. 32), NIS2, ISO 27001/27799, and eHealth Belgium requirements.
- Operational & patient safety risks → Potential errors in scheduling, admission, surgery planning, or record access.

These issues are amplified by growing clinical complexity and dynamic staffing models (rotations, on-call, consultants).

## Strategic Business Drivers 

1. Protect patient data & clinical integrity – Enforce least privilege and contextual access.
2. Achieve audit readiness & compliance – Full traceability for regulators and incident response.
3. Boost operational efficiency – Automate provisioning/deprovisioning via HR integrations (ULIS, Foederis).
4. Adopt Zero Trust by design – Continuous verification, no implicit trust.
5. Secure the end-to-end patient journey – From appointment to prescription and surgical intervention.
